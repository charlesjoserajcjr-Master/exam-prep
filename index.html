    Cargo

    Oil quantity and ullage, with trim and list

    ⏱ About 22 min · Part A compulsory
    A box-shaped cargo oil tank 30 m × 20 m × 15 m is to be loaded with crude oil at 24°C. Density of oil at 15°C in vacuum = 0.8550 kg/litre. If 4% of the tank volume is to be left for expansion, calculate the quantity of oil loaded and the final ullage by tape at the ullage port located 2 m forward of the aft bulkhead, 1 m above the tank top and 3 m to port of the tank centreline. LBP 240 m, trim 3 m by the stern, list ½° to starboard.MMD past paper, Apr 2026 · also Oct 2025, Jul 2023
    1. Volume to load (TOV at 24°C)
    Tank vol  = 30×20×15    = 9 000 m³
Expansion = 0.04×9 000  =   360 m³
TOV       = 9 000 − 360 = 8 640 m³
    2. Ullage on even keel, upright
    Oil depth = 8 640 ÷ 600  = 14.400 m
Ullage    = 15 − 14.400 =  0.600 m
From port = 0.600 + 1   =  1.600 m
    3. Trim correction (port is 13 m aft of tank centre)
    (trim ÷ LBP) × dist
= (3 ÷ 240) × 13 = 0.1625 m
→ subtract (port aft, trim by stern)
    4. List correction (port is 3 m to port, ship listed to stbd)
    dist × tan list
= 3 × tan 0.5° = 0.0262 m
→ add (port side is high)
    5. Final ullage
    1.6000 − 0.1625 + 0.0262 = 1.4637 ≈ 1.464 m

    Check: level change over half-length 0.19 m, half-breadth 0.09 m; surface is 0.60 m below the tank top, so it touches neither top nor bottom.

    6. Quantity
    VCF (54A, 0.8550, 24°C) = 0.9924
GSV = 8 640 × 0.9924   = 8 574.3 m³
Vac = 8 574.3 × 0.8550 = 7 331.0 t
WCF = 0.8550 − 0.0011  = 0.8539
Air = 8 574.3 × 0.8539 = 7 321.6 t
    Answer
    • Final ullage at the port = 1.464 m
    • Oil loaded = 8 640 m³ at 24°C = 8 574.3 m³ at 15°C = 7 331.0 t (vacuum) / 7 321.6 t (air)

    Examiner looks for: 4% of tank volume, port height added, trim distance from tank centre (not bulkhead), correct sign for each correction, VCF and WCF stated. Confirm VCF against the ASTM table given in the hall.

    TCN

    Great circle: distance, initial and final course

    ⏱ About 25 min · Section B compulsory
    Find the initial course, final course and great circle distance from 70°45'N 030°46'E to 40°00'N 110°15'E.MMD past paper, Apr 2026 · variant Jan 2026
    Sketch 1: spherical triangle PAB
    Sketch 2: the route on a Mercator chart

    Draw both in the exam. The triangle shows the examiner which sides and angles you used: ∠A is the initial course; ∠B is the bearing of A from B, which you reverse to get the final course. The chart sketch proves the courses make sense: the initial course is close to 090° because A is almost at the vertex.

    Lat A 70°45'N   Lat B 40°00'N
d.long = 110°15'E − 030°46'E = 79°29' E
    1. Distance (cosine formula)
    cos D = sin A·sin B + cos A·cos B·cos d.long
      = (0.94409 × 0.64279) + (0.32969 × 0.76604 × 0.18252)
      = 0.60685 + 0.04610 = 0.65295
D     = 49°14.2' = 2 954.2 n.m.
    2. Initial course (ABC at A)
    A = tan lat A ÷ tan d.long = 2.86356 ÷ 5.38677 = 0.5316 S
B = tan lat B ÷ sin d.long = 0.83910 ÷ 0.98320 = 0.8534 N
C = B − A = 0.3218 N
tan Az = 1 ÷ (C × cos lat A) = 1 ÷ (0.3218 × 0.32969) = 9.4242
IC = N 83°56.6' E = 083.9° (T)
    3. Final course (ABC at B towards A, then reverse)
    A = tan lat B ÷ tan d.long = 0.1558 S
B = tan lat A ÷ sin d.long = 2.9125 N
C = B − A = 2.7567 N
tan Az = 1 ÷ (2.7567 × 0.76604) = 0.4735  →  N 25°20.4' W
FC = reverse = S 25°20.4' E = 154.7° (T)
    4. Vertex (sense check)
    cos lat V = cos lat A × sin IC = 0.32969 × 0.99441
lat V = 70°51.7'N
tan d.long AV = 1 ÷ (sin lat A × tan IC) = 0.1124
d.long AV = 6°24.7' E  →  long V = 037°10.7'E
    Answer
    • Distance 2 954.2 n.m.
    • Initial course 083.9° (T)
    • Final course 154.7° (T)

    Examiner looks for: d.long named, formula written before numbers, ABC naming rules (A opposite to lat when d.long < 90°, B same as destination lat), final course reversed from the bearing at B.

    Safety

    Salient features of the ISPS Code

    ⏱ About 30 min · most-repeated Safety question
    What are the salient features of the ISPS Code to ensure the security of the ship's crew and passengers?MMD past paper, Apr 2026 · asked in 7 sessions since 2018
    Introduction

    The International Ship and Port Facility Security (ISPS) Code was adopted by IMO in December 2002 under SOLAS Chapter XI-2 and came into force on 1 July 2004. Part A is mandatory; Part B is guidance.

    Applies to: passenger ships (including HSC) and cargo ships of 500 GT and above on international voyages, MODUs, and the port facilities serving them.

    Salient features
    1. Three security levels, set by the Administration (ship) or port state (port):
      LevelOn board
      1 NormalGangway watch, ID checks, restricted areas locked
      2 HeightenedFewer access points, more patrols, search of persons, baggage and stores
      3 ExceptionalFollow authorities' instructions; may stop cargo work or move the ship
    2. Defined responsibilities: Company Security Officer (CSO) ashore, Ship Security Officer (SSO) on board, Port Facility Security Officer (PFSO). The Master has overriding authority for safety and security (SOLAS XI-2/8).
    3. Ship Security Assessment → Ship Security Plan: SSP approved by the Administration or an RSO; confidential; covers access control, restricted areas, cargo and stores handling, monitoring, response to threats and reporting.
    4. Access control and restricted areas: only identified persons board; visitors escorted; bridge, engine room, steering gear and CO₂ room secured.
    5. Screening and monitoring: baggage, stores and cargo checked; deck lighting, watchkeeping and alarms to detect intruders.
    6. Declaration of Security between ship and port (or ship and ship) when security levels differ or risk is higher.
    7. Supporting equipment: Ship Security Alert System (covert alert), AIS, permanently marked IMO number, Continuous Synopsis Record.
    8. Drills and training: security drills at least every 3 months; exercises at least once each calendar year, not more than 18 months apart; records kept on board.
    9. Certification: International Ship Security Certificate, valid up to 5 years with one intermediate verification.
    How this protects crew and passengers

    Unauthorised persons, weapons and dangerous devices are kept off the ship; threats are detected early; every crew member knows their duties at each level; and the ship can raise a covert alert if attacked.

    Conclusion

    The ISPS Code makes security a planned, audited shipboard function with clear roles, a ship-specific plan and trained crew.

    ≈ 380 words: about 1½–2 handwritten pages in 30 minutes. Draw the security level table; it earns marks quickly.
